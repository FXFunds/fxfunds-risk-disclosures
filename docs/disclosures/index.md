## Risks Common to All Markets

- **You can lose money.** Prices can move against you quickly, and losses may exceed what you expect.
- **Gaps and slippage.** Prices can jump between trades, especially at market open, after news or over weekends, so orders may fill at worse prices than requested.
- **Costs.** Spreads, commissions and overnight (swap) charges reduce returns and can change, for example spreads often widen during volatile periods.
- **Platform dependence.** Trading relies on the platform's systems, prices and execution; outages or delays can affect your positions.
- **Product form.** Many online platforms offer these markets as derivatives (such as CFDs) rather than direct ownership. If so, you do not own the underlying asset and the platform's terms decide how prices, dividends and corporate events are handled.

## Forex

Currency prices move with interest rates, economic data, central bank decisions and political events. Markets trade around the clock on weekdays but can gap at the weekly open. Most retail forex trading uses leverage, which increases risk.

## Stocks

Prices react to company results, news and corporate actions such as dividends, splits and mergers. Individual shares can fall sharply on a single announcement. Trading is limited to exchange hours, so prices can gap overnight.

## Indices

An index tracks a basket of shares, so it is affected by the broad market and by its largest members. Index prices can gap at the open after overnight news, and derivative index prices may differ slightly from the official index level.

## Commodities, Gold and Oil

Prices are driven by supply and demand, weather, inventories, geopolitics and currency moves. Oil can be extremely volatile: in April 2020 a US crude oil futures contract settled below zero. Prices offered online are often based on futures contracts, which can create rollover costs or price adjustments.

## Cryptocurrencies

Crypto markets trade 24 hours a day, every day, and can move sharply at any time, including when traditional markets are closed. Liquidity varies between tokens and venues. Prices can be affected by regulation, exchange failures, hacks and changes to the underlying networks. See also [cryptocurrency and stablecoin risks](../crypto-risks/index.md).

## Leverage and Margin

Leverage lets you open a position larger than your deposit. It magnifies both gains and losses.

| Leverage | Margin used | Adverse move that wipes out the margin |
|----------|-------------|----------------------------------------|
| 1:10 | 10% | 10% |
| 1:50 | 2% | 2% |
| 1:100 | 1% | 1% |
| 1:500 | 0.2% | 0.2% |

If your account equity falls below the required margin, positions may be closed automatically (stop-out), often at an unfavourable price. Check the platform's leverage limits, margin call and stop-out levels, and whether your balance can go negative, in its official terms. These were not located publicly at the last check.
